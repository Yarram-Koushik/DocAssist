Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Overview:
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is defined as progressive, irreversible kidney damage or sustained reduction in kidney function (estimated glomerular filtration rate, eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m²) lasting for three months or longer. The kidneys perform critical physiological roles: filtering metabolic waste products (urea, creatinine) and excess water from blood, balancing systemic electrolytes, regulating acid-base equilibrium, controlling blood pressure via the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, and producing erythropoietin for red blood cell synthesis.

Stages of Chronic Kidney Disease (NKF KDOQI Classification):
- Stage 1: Kidney damage with normal or elevated eGFR (>= 90 mL/min/1.73m²) and persistent albuminuria
- Stage 2: Mild reduction in eGFR (60–89 mL/min/1.73m²) with structural/urinary abnormalities
- Stage 3: Moderate reduction in eGFR (Stage 3a: 45–59 mL/min; Stage 3b: 30–44 mL/min)
- Stage 4: Severe reduction in eGFR (15–29 mL/min/1.73m²) – preparation for renal replacement
- Stage 5: Kidney failure / End-Stage Renal Disease (eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m² or requiring dialysis/transplantation)

Significance of Creatinine, eGFR, and Urine Albumin:
- Serum Creatinine: Waste product from muscle breakdown cleared by kidneys; rising blood levels indicate declining clearance.
- eGFR: Calculated using serum creatinine, age, and biological sex, serving as the gold-standard metric for kidney function.
- Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR): Detects early microvascular glomerular leakage (> 30 mg/g is abnormal).

Symptoms:
CKD is predominantly asymptomatic in Stages 1–3 ("silent progressive decline"). In advanced stages (Stages 4–5), uremic symptoms appear:
- Peripheral edema (swelling of feet, ankles, legs, hands, and periorbital tissue)
- Generalized fatigue, weakness, and cognitive clouding
- Nocturia, foamy or bubbly urine, or oliguria (decreased urine output)
- Intractable pruritus (severe itching), metallic taste, and ammonia breath odor
- Nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, and muscle cramps
- Refractory hypertension and dyspnea due to pulmonary fluid overload

Risk Factors:
- Diabetes mellitus (diabetic nephropathy, the leading cause of CKD)
- Chronic hypertension (hypertensive nephrosclerosis)
- Glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease (PKD), and recurring pyelonephritis
- Prolonged use of nephrotoxic medications (e.g., NSAIDs, certain antibiotics)
- Advanced age (> 60 years) and family history of kidney failure

Prevention and Management:
- Tight glycemic control in individuals with diabetes (target HbA1c < 7.0%)
- Strict blood pressure management (< 130/80 mmHg), often utilizing ACE inhibitors or ARBs for renoprotection
- Dietary modifications: Low-sodium (< 2,000 mg/day), moderate protein intake, and potassium/phosphorus monitoring in advanced stages
- Avoiding nephrotoxic agents (unsupervised high-dose NSAIDs) and smoking cessation

When to See a Doctor:
Undergo annual screening for eGFR and uACR if you have diabetes, hypertension, or cardiovascular disease. Seek immediate medical care if experiencing severe swelling, profound shortness of breath, sudden decrease in urination, or persistent vomiting.
