Heart Disease (Coronary Artery Disease)

Overview:
Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) is the most common form of cardiovascular disease and a leading cause of global mortality. CAD occurs when coronary arteries supplying oxygenated blood to the myocardium become narrowed or occluded by atherosclerotic plaques consisting of cholesterol, fat, calcium, and cellular waste products. Over time, plaque buildup reduces myocardial perfusion (ischemia) or can rupture, triggering acute thrombus formation and leading to acute myocardial infarction (heart attack).

Symptoms:
Many individuals remain asymptomatic until significant stenosis occurs. Common symptoms include:
- Angina pectoris: Pressure, fullness, squeezing, burning, or aching in the center of the chest, frequently radiating to the shoulders, arms (especially left arm), neck, jaw, back, or epigastrium
- Shortness of breath (dyspnea) during exertion or at rest
- Extreme fatigue during routine daily activities
- Dizziness, lightheadedness, or unexplained weakness

Symptoms of an Acute Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack):
- Crushing retrosternal chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes or resolving and returning
- Pain radiating down the left arm, shoulder, throat, jaw, or upper abdomen
- Sudden cold sweating (diaphoresis), nausea, vomiting, or lightheadedness
- Unexplained sudden severe shortness of breath
- Note: Women, diabetics, and older adults may present with atypical symptoms, such as sharp back pain, profound exhaustion, nausea, or dizziness without severe chest pain.

Risk Factors:
- Major Modifiable: Elevated low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, cigarette smoking, physical inactivity, obesity (abdominal adiposity), and diets high in trans/saturated fats
- Non-Modifiable: Advancing age, male sex (or post-menopausal females), and family history of premature atherosclerotic coronary disease

Prevention:
- Adopting a heart-healthy diet (Mediterranean or DASH diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, omega-3 fatty acids, and low in sodium and saturated fats)
- Engaging in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week
- Strict smoking cessation and avoiding second-hand smoke exposure
- Rigorous control of blood pressure, blood glucose, and lipid profiles through lifestyle adjustments and clinician-directed pharmacotherapy

When to See a Doctor:
Call emergency services immediately (911 or local emergency number) if you or someone else experiences symptoms suggestive of a heart attack. Do not drive yourself to the emergency department. Schedule a medical consultation if you experience recurrent exertion-related chest tightness, palpitations, or decreased exercise tolerance.
