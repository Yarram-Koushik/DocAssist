Anemia

Overview:
Anemia is a common hematologic condition characterized by a deficiency in the number of healthy red blood cells (erythrocytes) or a decreased concentration of hemoglobin within them. Hemoglobin is the iron-rich protein responsible for binding and delivering oxygen from the lungs to tissues throughout the body. When hemoglobin levels are depressed, cellular oxygenation is compromised, impairing organ function and energy metabolism.

Common Types of Anemia:
- Iron-Deficiency Anemia: The most prevalent form worldwide, caused by insufficient dietary iron, poor iron absorption, chronic gastrointestinal bleeding, or increased physiological demand (e.g., pregnancy, rapid adolescent growth).
- Vitamin Deficiency Anemia (Megaloblastic Anemia): Caused by inadequate levels of vitamin B12 (cobalamin) or folate (vitamin B9), resulting in abnormally large, structurally dysfunctional red blood cells. Pernicious anemia is an autoimmune subtype preventing B12 absorption.
- Anemia of Chronic Disease: Associated with chronic inflammation, infections, autoimmune diseases, or malignancies.
- Hemolytic and Aplastic Anemias: Resulting from accelerated erythrocyte destruction or bone marrow suppression.

Symptoms:
- Persistent, overwhelming fatigue and generalized weakness
- Pallor (paleness of the skin, conjunctiva, nail beds, and gums)
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath) on mild exertion
- Dizziness, lightheadedness, and orthostatic instability
- Cold hands and feet (peripheral vasoconstriction)
- Tachycardia, irregular heartbeat, or palpitations
- Headache, brittle spoon-shaped nails (koilonychia), and sore or smooth tongue (glossitis)
- Pica (craving non-nutritive substances such as ice, clay, or starch)

Dietary Sources and Prevention:
- Heme Iron (high bioavailability): Lean red meats, poultry, fish, and organ meats
- Non-Heme Iron: Spinach, legumes, lentils, fortified cereals, tofu, and pumpkin seeds (enhanced when consumed with vitamin C)
- Vitamin B12: Dairy products, eggs, fish, meat, and B12-fortified plant milks
- Folate: Leafy green vegetables, citrus fruits, beans, peas, and enriched grains

Risk Factors:
- Inadequate nutritional intake or strict vegan/vegetarian diets without appropriate supplementation
- Chronic blood loss (heavy menstrual periods, gastrointestinal ulcers, colon polyps)
- Malabsorption syndromes (celiac disease, Crohn's disease, gastric bypass surgery)
- Pregnancy and chronic illnesses (kidney disease, rheumatoid arthritis)

When to See a Doctor:
Schedule a medical consultation if you experience persistent unexplained fatigue, dizziness, or shortness of breath. Seek emergency medical care if experiencing chest pain, rapid irregular pulse, or fainting.
